04

Cryptography Concepts

Understanding the theory behind Caesar cipher and classical cryptography.

🔒

Encryption

Encryption transforms readable plaintext into an unreadable ciphertext using a key. The Caesar cipher encrypts by shifting each letter forward by K positions in the alphabet.

C = (P + K) mod 26
  • P = plaintext letter position (A=0, Z=25)
  • K = the shift key (0–25)
  • C = ciphertext letter position
  • Non-alphabetic characters remain unchanged
Example (K=3): 'A' → (0+3) mod 26 = 3 → 'D'
🔓

Decryption

Decryption is the reverse of encryption — it restores ciphertext back to plaintext. With Caesar cipher, you simply shift letters backward by K positions.

P = (C − K + 26) mod 26
  • Adding 26 prevents negative modulo values
  • Decryption requires knowing the correct key K
  • Without the key, brute force is needed
  • Case is preserved during decryption
Example (K=3): 'D' → (3−3+26) mod 26 = 0 → 'A'
🗝️

Keys & Key Space

The key is the secret value that controls how encryption and decryption work. The Caesar cipher has a very small key space of only 26 possible keys.

|Key Space| = 26
  • K=0 → No change (identity cipher)
  • K=13 → ROT-13 (self-inverse)
  • K=25 → Same as K=−1 (shift back 1)
  • Modern ciphers have 2256 keys!
ROT-13 property: Encrypt(Encrypt(x)) = x
⚡

Brute-Force Attack

A brute-force attack tries every possible key until the correct one is found. Because Caesar cipher has only 26 keys, it can be broken instantly — making it completely insecure.

Time ∝ |Key Space| = 26 attempts
  • Attack tries all 26 shifts systematically
  • Human reads results to find English plaintext
  • Frequency analysis can narrow candidates
  • Modern ciphers require 2128+ attempts
Security level: Negligible — cracked in < 1 second
🏛️

History

Named after Julius Caesar (~100 BC – 44 BC), who reportedly used a shift of 3 to communicate secretly with his generals during military campaigns.

  • Described by Suetonius in "The Twelve Caesars"
  • One of the earliest known ciphers
  • Basis for later Vigenère & substitution ciphers
  • Still used in ROT-13 for online spoiler text
Modern use: ROT-13 on Reddit/Usenet for spoilers
📊

Frequency Analysis

In English, the letter 'E' appears ~13% of the time. Since Caesar cipher preserves letter frequency, the most frequent ciphertext letter reveals the key — without brute force!

  • Most common English letters: E, T, A, O, I
  • Find most frequent ciphertext letter = E
  • Key = (cipher_position − 4) mod 26
  • Works reliably on messages > 100 chars
